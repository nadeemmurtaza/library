# Collage UI design style

Cutout photography, overlapping fragments, mixed scales, torn edges, and layered media create expressive compositions.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Collage became a named modern-art practice when Picasso and Braque began attaching paper and other found material to Cubist compositions around 1912. Dada, Surrealism, punk, editorial design, and digital image tools repeatedly extended the technique, so today’s UI collage mixes photography, scans, cut edges, and type within a lineage of deliberate recombination.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Pull a small palette from the source photographs, then unify mixed materials with repeated paper tones, ink colors, or one overlay wash. In a daisyUI theme, use the recurring ink or overlay-wash hue for `primary`, a strong photo-derived color for `secondary`, and assign clean paper, aged paper, and shadowed paper tones to `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300`.

Mix cutout photography, scans, handwriting, found print, paint, and torn paper while preserving visible edges and source texture.

Allow high visual density near focal compositions, but keep copy concise and surround important actions with uncluttered paper space.

Assemble pieces through pasted entrances, small parallax offsets, torn-mask reveals, and stop-frame rearrangements that retain a handmade feel.

Use torn contours, taped rectangles, rough circles, irregular masks, and cropped fragments rather than polished component silhouettes. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables to `0` or `0.25rem`.

Mix at most two contrasting display voices with one dependable reading face, using found type for accents and never for essential instructions.
