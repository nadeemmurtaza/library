# Cyberdelia UI design style

Cyberculture merges with psychedelia through acid color, fractals, kaleidoscopic imagery, early 3D type, and rave-era digital optimism.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Cyberdelia joined psychedelic color and imagery with the first broadly visible wave of digital manipulation, fractals, rave graphics, and network mythology. CARI places its relevance from the late 1980s to mid-1990s and notes that the name became widely recognizable through the Cyberdelia club in the 1995 film Hackers; the visual tendency predates that film.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use acid green, ultraviolet, cyan, hot orange, black, and spectral gradients in luminous combinations that resemble projected club graphics. In a daisyUI theme, use acid green or ultraviolet for `primary`, cyan or hot orange for `secondary`, and step `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` through deep indigo, dark violet, and black.

Choose fractals, kaleidoscopes, chrome insects, cybernetic eyes, wireframes, early CGI, rave footage, and psychedelic computer diagrams.

Use high visual density but low to medium copy, keeping event or product information on stable overlays above the shifting cyber-psychedelic field.

Travel through kaleidoscopic tunnels, iterate fractals, rotate cybernetic forms, and pulse patterns to rhythm without unsafe flashing.

Use spirals, fractal branches, cyber grids, liquid rings, early-three-dimensional glyphs, and kaleidoscopic radial symmetry. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `1rem` and `2rem`.

Use bulbous or early-3D display lettering for short names, paired with a crisp digital sans or mono that remains readable over moving color.
