# Streamline Moderne UI design style

Aerodynamic curves, horizontal bands, chrome, rounded corners, and machine-age speed simplify Art Deco ornament.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Streamline Moderne was the machine-age, aerodynamic branch of late Art Deco. Designers translated research and imagery from aircraft, ships, trains, and wind tunnels into rounded corners, horizontal bands, smooth shells, and chrome details—even for stationary products—and the style reached a mass audience at fairs such as the 1939–1940 New York World’s Fair.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use cream, navy, sea green, signal red, and polished chrome, with restrained gradients that suggest enamel, speed, and machinery. In a daisyUI theme, use navy or sea green for `primary`, signal red for `secondary`, and assign cream, pale silver, and cool light gray to `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300`.

Choose ocean liners, trains, automobiles, terminals, appliances, and aerodynamic industrial details photographed or illustrated from low sweeping angles.

Use medium density arranged in long horizontal groups, keeping vertical clutter low and prioritizing routes, features, or destinations.

Glide panels laterally, trail restrained speed lines, and sweep highlights along curved surfaces with smooth constant acceleration.

Favor aerodynamic capsules, horizontal ribs, portholes, rounded corners, teardrops, and chrome bands stripped of ornate Deco detail. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.75rem` and `1.5rem`.

Use a streamlined geometric or condensed sans-serif with broad horizontal spacing, keeping labels compact and aligned to the motion bands.
