# Art Deco UI design style

Symmetrical geometry, stepped forms, luxurious metallic accents, dramatic contrast, and ornamental typography.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Art Deco developed from prewar French luxury design and received its defining international showcase at the 1925 Exposition internationale des arts décoratifs et industriels modernes in Paris. It combined craft and expensive materials with stepped geometry, streamlined machinery, and imagery drawn from many cultures, then spread through architecture, transport, fashion, cinema, and graphic design.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Combine black or deep jewel tones with ivory and controlled gold, silver, or brass accents, maintaining crisp high-contrast separation. In a daisyUI theme, use controlled gold or brass for `primary`, emerald, sapphire, or oxblood for `secondary`, and step `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` through deep charcoal, ink black, and jewel-tinted black.

Use dramatic architecture, polished products, theatrical portraiture, fan motifs, and geometric city or stage imagery with hard directional light.

Keep density low to medium, allowing metallic linework and monumental headings to frame a concise premium message.

Reveal fan patterns radially, move through stepped masks, and add brief metallic glints or curtain-like openings with measured theatrical timing.

Use sunbursts, fans, chevrons, stepped pyramids, fluted lines, octagons, and symmetrical frames with sharp geometric precision. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables to `0` or `0.25rem`.

Use geometric display capitals with generous tracking for short headings, paired with a restrained serif or sans-serif for details and controls.
