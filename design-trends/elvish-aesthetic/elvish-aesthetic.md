# Elvish Aesthetic UI design style

Nature-bound high fantasy combines graceful verticality, flowing botanical ornament, luminous landscapes, refined handcraft, and an ancient otherworldly calm.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Elvish Aesthetic is a modern fantasy category, not the art of a historical people. Its visual language combines medieval manuscript and architectural references, Pre-Raphaelite and Arts and Crafts medievalism, Art Nouveau nature forms, and the languages, calligraphy, maps, and descriptions developed by J. R. R. Tolkien; later illustration, role-playing games, and cinema made that synthesis globally familiar.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use forest green, moss, ivory, moonlit blue, silver, and pale gold with restrained jewel accents, choosing either a sunlit woodland or cool starlit mood for each view. In a daisyUI theme, use forest green for `primary`, moonlit blue or pale gold for `secondary`, and assign ivory, misted sage, and pale stone to `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` for a sunlit woodland scheme.

Show ancient forests, waterfalls, tree-grown halls, hand-carved wood, fine metalwork, bows, stars, maps, and serene otherworldly figures; invent original heraldry rather than copying a known fantasy world.

Keep density low to medium, giving scenery and ornament room to breathe while revealing lore, inventories, or navigation in clear layered panels.

Grow vine lines, trace star paths, shimmer metal or water, drift leaves and motes, and reveal tall spaces with slow weightless motion.

Favor elongated arches, leaf points, whiplash curves, interlaced branches, crescents, teardrops, and fine filigree instead of heavy boxes. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.75rem` and `1.5rem`.

Pair an elegant literary serif with a calligraphic or uncial display face for short names, and keep body text plain; never replace essential labels with invented runes.
