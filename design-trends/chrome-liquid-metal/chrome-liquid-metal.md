# Chrome / Liquid Metal UI design style

Mirror-like metallic surfaces, molten forms, sharp reflections, and high-contrast specular lighting.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Chrome and liquid-metal UI is a contemporary digital treatment, not one continuous historic movement. Its polished surfaces recall Streamline Moderne’s 1930s celebration of machines and speed, later chrome-heavy airbrush and music graphics, and the accessible 3D rendering tools that drove a new metallic branding cycle in the early 2020s.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Build from black, gunmetal, silver, and blown white highlights, then introduce one hot accent such as electric blue, racing red, or acid green. In a daisyUI theme, use the chosen hot accent for `primary`, gunmetal or cool silver for `secondary`, and make `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` a descending stack of graphite, near-black, and black.

Use photoreal molten chrome, polished machinery, reflective products, and studio imagery with deliberately visible strip lights and hard specular edges.

Keep the opening experience sparse and dramatic, then shift detailed specifications or commerce content into quieter dark panels.

Sweep controlled reflections across surfaces, rotate objects slowly, and morph metal contours with heavy, viscous timing.

Use liquid capsules, aerodynamic cuts, embossed plates, and smooth metallic bulges contrasted against razor-sharp framing lines. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.75rem` and `1.5rem`.

Choose extended geometric display faces, use embossed or mirrored treatments only at headline scale, and set technical copy in a restrained neutral sans-serif.
