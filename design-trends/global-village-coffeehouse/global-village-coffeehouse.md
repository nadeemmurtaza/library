# Global Village Coffeehouse UI design style

Earth tones, rough borders, woodcut-like symbols, suns, moons, spirals, natural motifs, and sincere hand-drawn globalism.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Global Village Coffeehouse is a retrospective name for warm commercial graphics that combined hand drawing, earth tones, woodcut-like marks, suns, moons, globes, spirals, and an idealized multicultural “global village.” CARI places its peak in the mid-1990s and documents the label as a 2017 research-community coinage; its source imagery also deserves scrutiny for flattening distinct cultures into marketable motifs.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use terracotta, ochre, forest, indigo, coffee brown, faded turquoise, and unbleached paper with matte imperfect coverage. In a daisyUI theme, use terracotta or forest for `primary`, faded turquoise or ochre for `secondary`, and set `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` to unbleached paper, warm tan, and light coffee beige.

Choose woodcut-like suns, moons, hands, spirals, plants, maps, food, craft, and sincere multicultural community scenes with contextual care.

Use medium to high detail in labeled groups, balancing pattern and storytelling with straightforward menus, schedules, or service information.

Draw rough borders and symbols on, rotate suns slowly, and reveal handcrafted motifs with tactile unhurried transitions.

Favor rough borders, spirals, rays, hand-cut circles, folk symbols, vines, and irregular framed panels with visible craft. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.25rem` and `1rem`.

Use a warm humanist serif or sans-serif with hand-lettered accents, avoiding faux-ethnic display fonts and maintaining clear practical labels.
