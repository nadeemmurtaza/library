# Holographic / Iridescent UI design style

Spectral color shifts, pearlescent gradients, reflective surfaces, diffraction, and futuristic light effects.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Holographic UI is a current visual label rather than a direct use of holography: most screens imitate iridescence with animated gradients and specular color. Its vocabulary borrows from physical holography, invented by Dennis Gabor in 1947, and from diffraction films and metallic fashion that became common source material for late-2010s digital design.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use angle-shifting cyan, violet, rose, and lime across pearlescent highlights, grounded by charcoal, white, or soft gray fields. In a daisyUI theme, use cyan-violet for `primary`, rose or restrained lime for `secondary`, and ground `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` in white, pearl gray, and soft cool gray or in tightly stepped charcoals.

Favor macro shots of iridescent film, reflective packaging, translucent three-dimensional forms, and prismatic light captured against simple backgrounds.

Maintain low to moderate density, leaving dark breathing room around reflective focal elements and avoiding rainbow treatment on every component.

Tie spectral shifts to pointer angle, scroll position, or object rotation; use slow shimmer passes rather than constant random hue cycling.

Mix smooth discs and inflated foil membranes with a few faceted prisms, keeping functional containers simpler than decorative objects. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.75rem` and `1.5rem`.

Use wide futuristic display type for short statements, pair it with a neutral sans-serif body, and restrict iridescent fills to large readable letterforms.
