# Cassette Futurism UI design style

Chunky analog controls, CRT displays, industrial panels, physical switches, amber or green screens, and late-20th-century science-fiction technology.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Cassette Futurism is a retrospective internet label for futures imagined with tape decks, CRTs, membrane keys, switches, and bulky modular hardware. CARI places its source period from the mid-1970s to late 1980s and records the name as a later coinage on TV Tropes, meaning the original production designers did not generally use it for their own work.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use charcoal, military olive, beige plastic, and worn metal with amber or green phosphor displays and small red warning indicators. In a daisyUI theme, use amber or green phosphor for `primary`, military olive for `secondary`, and step `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` through charcoal, near-black metal, and black.

Use chunky switches, tape decks, CRT readouts, cables, vents, industrial labels, and late-century spacecraft or computer hardware.

Support high density through clearly separated physical panels, engraved legends, and dedicated readout zones rather than floating digital cards. Use the `*-sm` size variants for daisyUI components to preserve this compact working density.

Use CRT boot sequences, scan refresh, tape motion, switch snaps, relay delays, and occasional signal dropout with believable hardware response.

Favor boxy housings, vents, recessed screens, chunky toggles, rotary knobs, handles, screws, and thick molded bezels. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.25rem` and `1rem`.

Use monospaced phosphor text for displays and condensed industrial lettering for panel labels, keeping terminology terse and mechanically aligned.

### Skeuomorphism UI design style

Photorealistic or softly modeled interfaces use material depth, mechanical controls, and analog-equipment cues, often combining industrial nostalgia with clean retro-futurist geometry.

### Cyberpunk UI design style

Dense dark interfaces use neon accents, glitches, terminal typography, surveillance motifs, and futuristic HUD elements.

### Y2K Techno-Skeuomorphism UI design style

Early-2000s skinnable media-player interfaces model software after futuristic audio equipment; the style is also recognized as Y2K Media Player UI, Chrome Futurism, Skinnable UI, Techno Skeuomorphism, or Millennium Futurism.
