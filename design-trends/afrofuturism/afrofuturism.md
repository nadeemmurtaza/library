# Afrofuturism UI design style

African and diasporic cultural references combine with speculative technology, cosmic imagery, bold pattern, and future-facing storytelling.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Cultural critic Mark Dery coined “Afrofuturism” in 1993, but the work it names is much older. Writers and thinkers including W. E. B. Du Bois, musicians such as Sun Ra, and many later artists used speculative futures, technology, African histories, and diasporic experience to examine liberation and identity; the field cannot be reduced to a decorative sci-fi motif set.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use deep black, indigo, royal purple, earth, gold, copper, and electric accents informed by the specific African or diasporic context. In a daisyUI theme, use context-appropriate royal purple or indigo for `primary`, gold or copper for `secondary`, and step `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` through dark earth, near-black, and deep black.

Combine culturally grounded textiles, architecture, adornment, portraiture, ancestral symbols, landscapes, and speculative machines without treating traditions as interchangeable.

Use medium to high symbolic density, supporting each motif with enough context and leaving practical information in clearly structured regions.

Unfold patterns, orbit cosmic elements, activate metallic circuitry, and transition between ancestral and future imagery with ceremonial pacing.

Draw geometry from the chosen culture's pattern, craft, architecture, and symbolism, combining it with stars, rings, shields, and engineered forms. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.25rem` and `1rem`.

Choose bold contemporary display type that respects the cultural reference, pair it with a highly readable sans-serif, and avoid invented pseudo-African lettering.
