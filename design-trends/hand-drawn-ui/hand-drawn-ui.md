# Hand-Drawn UI design style

Imperfect lines, sketchy icons, paper textures, handwritten type, and playful annotations emphasize human craft.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Hand-Drawn UI is a technique category, not a dated movement. Its claim to visible human authorship echoes the Arts and Crafts reaction against industrial standardization in late-19th-century Britain, while scanners, tablets, and vector tools later made imperfect lines and lettering practical components of digital products.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Work from off-white paper and dark ink, adding a small crayon, marker, or watercolor palette with visible but controlled variation. In a daisyUI theme, use the clearest marker or crayon hue for `primary`, a complementary watercolor tone for `secondary`, and map off-white paper, warm sketch paper, and lightly smudged gray paper to `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300`.

Choose line drawings, annotated diagrams, imperfect icons, and scanned marks that share one hand, tool, and stroke character.

Keep density moderate, leaving breathing room around strokes and preventing annotations from crossing labels or interactive targets.

Draw lines on, add tiny idle wobble to illustrations, and erase or redraw state changes with brief imperfect timing.

Use uneven outlines, hand-drawn boxes, circles, arrows, underlines, and paper edges with deliberate human variance. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.25rem` and `1rem`.

Use handwritten type for headings and callouts, pair it with a clean body face for longer reading, and avoid shrinking irregular letters below comfortable sizes.
