# Superflat Pop UI design style

Extremely flat perspective, bright candy color, dense cartoon motifs, polished outlines, and Japanese pop-cultural energy.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Takashi Murakami articulated Superflat in a 2000 manifesto and exhibition, using visual flatness to connect Japanese art history, manga, anime, consumer culture, and the collapse of “high” and “low” categories. Superflat Pop names the style’s especially commercial, character-driven expansion through fashion, animation, motion graphics, and products; it should remain connected to Murakami’s critical theory, not reduced to colorful cartoon flatness.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use bright candy pink, red, yellow, cyan, green, and purple with clean black or colored outlines and little atmospheric shading. In a daisyUI theme, use candy pink or red for `primary`, cyan or yellow for `secondary`, and set `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` to clean white, a pale candy tint, and light cool gray.

Choose polished cartoon characters, flowers, eyes, icons, consumer objects, anime-influenced motifs, and repeated decorative figures.

Embrace high visual density with medium copy, reserving simple flat color pockets for product details, prices, and controls.

Pan across flat scenes, tile motifs, pop characters into place, and swap expressions or colors with crisp two-dimensional timing.

Use flat contours, rounded character silhouettes, flowers, eyes, stars, bubbles, and repeated emblematic forms without perspective depth. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.75rem` and `1.5rem`.

Use bold rounded Japanese-pop display type for short headings, paired with a highly legible Gothic or sans-serif body and carefully set multilingual text.
