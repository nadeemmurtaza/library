# Surf Crush UI design style

The 1999–2008 surf-media craze combines sunlit ocean photography, boards, Hawaiian prints, puka shells, hibiscus flowers, beach hair, and youthful coastal freedom.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Surf Crush is a retrospective label for the hibiscus prints, turquoise water, puka shells, board shorts, and sunlit beach branding common around the turn of the millennium. Community histories place its source period around 1999–2008 and attribute the name to Steves Peeps in 2018, so it should be read as a later archive category rather than an original industry term.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Use ocean blue, aqua, sun yellow, hibiscus orange or pink, sand, tan, and white under bright natural daylight, avoiding both muted nautical restraint and sugary coquette pastels. In a daisyUI theme, use ocean blue or aqua for `primary`, hibiscus orange or sun yellow for `secondary`, and set `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300` to white, sunlit sand, and pale tan.

Show active surfing, swimming, volleyball, friend groups, boards, shells, beach hair, bonfires, palm-lined shores, and period surfwear with an honest outdoor snapshot quality.

Keep visual density lively and copy moderate, giving schedules, locations, conditions, products, and booking actions uncluttered contrast over the beach imagery.

Sweep sections like breaking waves, slide boards and snapshots into place, flutter fabric patterns, and use brief sun glints or water ripples without turning the scene into a tropical screensaver.

Use long surfboard ovals, curling wave arcs, hibiscus silhouettes, shells, sunbursts, torn photo edges, and repeating boardshort patterns. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.75rem` and `1.5rem`.

Combine chunky surfwear sans-serif or hand-painted display lettering with a relaxed readable body face, using scripts only as short sun-faded accents.
