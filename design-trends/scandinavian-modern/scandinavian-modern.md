# Scandinavian Modern UI design style

Functional simplicity, light palettes, warm natural materials, clean lines, and friendly human-centered restraint.

## Design overview

## History and origins

A fact-checked account of where this visual language came from, with links to the references used.

Scandinavian Modern developed through Nordic architecture, furniture, glass, textile, and product design, with roots in the 1920s and strong international visibility after World War II. It joined functional production with human scale, craft knowledge, light, and natural materials; “Danish Modern” became a particularly influential export within the broader regional story.

## Design principles and implementation

Use these principles to write a focused AI UI design prompt instead of asking for a vague visual treatment.

Choose white, soft gray, oatmeal, pale wood, muted blue, and forest or clay accents, keeping the overall light level high and natural. In a daisyUI theme, use muted blue or forest green for `primary`, clay for `secondary`, and assign white, oatmeal, and pale wood-gray to `base-100`, `base-200`, and `base-300`.

Use daylight interiors, honest material details, useful objects, landscapes, and relaxed human moments with minimal staging.

Keep density low to medium, favoring practical clarity, concise descriptions, and enough open space to convey ease and order.

Use subtle fades, short slides, and quiet hover feedback with natural timing, avoiding decorative loops or dramatic depth effects.

Favor clean lines, simple rectangles, modest soft corners, fine dividers, and occasional warm material blocks. In daisyUI themes, set the `--radius-*` variables between `0.25rem` and `1rem`.

Choose a friendly humanist sans-serif with an optional restrained serif accent, using comfortable sizes, open counters, and uncomplicated hierarchy.
